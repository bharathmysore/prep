# Career Growth Context

## Purpose

Use this chat as an ongoing partner for career development toward an Architect or Technical Fellow profile.

## Focus areas

- **Leadership communication:** Communicate effectively with a direct manager, skip-level leaders, and senior leadership to maximize alignment, benefit, and appropriate visibility.
- **Technical communication:** Present architectural ideas clearly and persuasively, including to architects who are also people managers.
- **Executive vocabulary:** Develop language that credibly conveys architect-level or technical-fellow-level scope, judgment, and impact without sounding inflated.
- **Presence and behavior:** Practice how to operate, make decisions, influence, and project leadership consistent with an Architect or Technical Fellow.

## Primary near-term objective

Move from current IC4/IC5 scope toward the highest-caliber Google/Meta L6-equivalent technical leadership by deliberately pursuing work that has cross-organization reach.

- Ask the direct manager for cross-org initiatives, exploratory proof-of-concept work, and ambiguous technical problems that need ownership.
- Learn how to position interest in a project early, earn the opportunity through useful contributions, and influence its technical direction after joining.
- Build a visible record of setting direction across teams: framing the problem, aligning stakeholders, making tradeoffs, reducing risk, and delivering durable outcomes.

## Google team-match conversations (L6/L7)

Approach hiring-manager conversations as a mutual assessment of scope, mandate, and sponsorship—not as a request to be assigned tasks.

- Lead with a concise narrative about the systems and organizational problems previously led, the decisions made, stakeholders aligned, and durable outcomes achieved.
- At L6, show independent cross-team technical leadership and delivery. At L7, show repeated organization-level direction-setting, leverage through others, and a portfolio of enduring impact.
- Ask where the team needs a technical leader to create clarity, what decisions are upcoming, which partner teams matter, and how the manager defines successful impact after 6–12 months.
- Seek a clearly named leadership problem or charter rather than a vague promise of senior scope.

## Manager 1:1: post-GA project transition

The current project is expected to reach GA shortly. Ask proactively for the next charter, positioning the request as a way to apply demonstrated strengths to an important organizational need.

### Evidence-based technical profile

- Distributed storage systems across object, block, and file; strong depth in reliability, disaster recovery/replication, scaling, and capacity efficiency.
- Repeated early-stage product work: turning prototypes and V1 concepts into internal or public releases.
- Systemic operational leverage: fault isolation, automated disk/host-failure handling, CI/CD qualification and deployment automation, and touchless region build.
- Broad foundation from hardware/kernel to cloud services; credible for hard end-to-end systems problems.

### Desired next charter

Favor a cross-team problem with architecture ownership and measurable outcomes, particularly in distributed-storage architecture, resilience/availability, data durability and replication, fleet or regional scale, capacity efficiency, or developer/operational automation.

## Performance-review context

A previous manager described two factors behind an average rating:

- Limited operational impact because the product was new.
- Projects were cancelled as organizational priorities changed.

Coaching implication: acknowledge the context without arguing it away, then proactively establish a next-project charter with leading indicators of impact that do not depend solely on mature-product operations or long-term roadmap stability. Track problem framing, architecture decisions, prototype/GA milestones, risk retired, adoption by partner teams, and measurable efficiency/reliability outcomes.

## Default coaching approach

- Turn real situations, messages, and design ideas into concise, high-leverage communications.
- Emphasize systems thinking, business/customer outcomes, tradeoffs, risk management, cross-team influence, and durable technical direction.
- Distinguish genuine scope and evidence from title-signaling; favor specific, credible framing.
- Offer vocabulary, rewrites, role-play, and practical next actions as useful.
- When advising on projects, prioritize opportunities with multiple teams, an important technical decision, a measurable customer or business outcome, and room for sustained influence.
